Narrative timeline

Past Perfect → earlier past event

C1 reminder: use Past Perfect when the earlier order needs clarification; don't force it into every past sentence.

Editing tools

Use Passive Voice when the result, process or object matters more than the person doing the action.

6. Case Reading — The Locked Chapter 🗝️

At eleven forty-five, Mara returned to the old publishing house to collect a notebook she had forgotten after the launch party. The building should have been empty, but a light was still on in the archive room.

When she opened the door, Daniel, the editor, was standing beside a locked cabinet. Papers were scattered across the floor. He told her that a pipe had burst and that he had been trying to save old manuscripts. His explanation sounded reasonable, but Mara noticed that the floor was completely dry.

Earlier that evening, the author of the new bestseller had made a strange joke about “a story that had been stolen twice.” Everyone had laughed. Mara had assumed it was simply part of the author’s dramatic personality.

Daniel asked Mara to leave. While he was speaking, she noticed a torn page under his shoe. It contained a paragraph she recognised from the bestseller — but the paper looked decades old.

The next morning, the publishing company announced that Daniel had resigned for personal reasons. The author gave no interviews. A week later, Mara received an anonymous envelope containing a photograph of Daniel as a teenager. He was standing beside the original writer of the manuscript.

Only then did Mara understand the joke from the launch party. The bestseller had not copied an old manuscript. The old manuscript had been written by Daniel’s mother, and Daniel had secretly rewritten it years later under another person's name. The author had discovered the truth before the party and had been trying to force Daniel to confess.
