Pick One… Or Neither ⚖️✨

Review today’s grammar for choices and preferences, practise the new vocabulary, and learn one extra structure we did not get to in class: whether…or.

Lesson Recap — Choice Grammar 🧠

Either…or = one of two

either A or B

Use it when both options are possible, but you choose one.

We can either stay home or go out.

Neither…nor = not A, not B

neither A nor B

Use it when both options are negative / rejected.

Neither the beach nor the city sounds relaxing today.

Both…and = A + B

both A and B

Use it when you want or like both options.

I like both quiet weekends and spontaneous trips.

Either / Neither / Both + of

either / neither / both of + plural group

Use of before: the / these / my / us / them.

Neither of the hotels is cheap. · Both of them look good.

Would rather = this choice now

would rather + base verb

No to after rather.

I’d rather stay home. · I’d rather fly than drive.

Prefer = general preference

prefer A to B

Use it for what you usually like more.

I prefer quiet evenings to hectic nights.

Agreement

Positive: So do I. / Me too.

Negative: Neither do I. / Me neither.

“I don’t like crowded airports.” — “Neither do I.”

NEW: Whether…or

whether A or B

Use it when there are two possibilities and the choice/result is still uncertain.

I haven’t decided whether to drive or fly.
Whether we stay home or go out, I want a low-key evening.
Fast memory trick: either = 1 of 2 · neither = 0 of 2 · both = 2 of 2 · rather = choice now · prefer = general preference · whether = “which possibility?”

6. Mini Reading — The Weekend Decision ☕🚗

Maria and her husband have had a hectic week, so they want to do something relaxing. Maria would rather stay somewhere quiet, but her husband prefers city breaks to countryside trips. They considered two hotels, but neither of them was convenient because both were far from the train station. Finally, they reached a compromise: they will take a scenic drive to a small town and treat themselves to dinner. They still haven’t decided whether to stay overnight or drive home the same evening. Either option is possible, and both agree that the trip will be worth it.
