Past Simple vs Past Continuous 🎬

Forms, uses, signal words, WHEN / WHILE, spelling and the difference between a completed event and an action in progress.

📸 Past Simple = Snapshot

V2 / verb + -ed

Use for:

  • finished action: I called yesterday.
  • sequence: I arrived, ordered and sat down.
  • past fact/state: The room was quiet.
  • main events in a story.

🎥 Past Continuous = Video

was / were + verb-ing

Use for:

  • action in progress at a past moment: At 5, I was working.
  • background scene: The sun was shining.
  • temporary past situation: I was staying with friends that month.
  • two simultaneous ongoing actions.

Core difference: Past Simple = “What happened?” · Past Continuous = “What was happening at that moment?”

When they appear together

The longer/background action is often Past Continuous and the shorter event is Past Simple:

I was riding home when it started to rain.

While I was cooking, the phone rang.

This pattern is common, but it is not the only use of either tense.

WHEN vs WHILE

when often introduces the shorter event: I was walking when it started to rain.

while often introduces the ongoing action: While I was walking, it started to rain.

Past Simple spelling

  • work → worked
  • live → lived
  • study → studied
  • stop → stopped

-ed sounds: /t/ worked · /d/ played · /ɪd/ wanted

Past Continuous spelling

  • work → was working
  • make → was making
  • run → was running
  • lie → was lying

Stative verbs

Usually stay simple even in a past scene: I knew, wanted, believed, understood, needed.

Not usually: I was knowing.

Meaning over signal words

Signal words are clues, not automatic rules.

At 5 p.m. I called him is correct if the call happened at that time as a completed event.

Set the scene

Use Past Continuous for what was already happening around the main event.

People were talking. Music was playing. I was waiting near the door.

Move the story forward

Use Past Simple for completed events in sequence.

The waiter arrived, gave us the menu and took our order.

Two ongoing actions

Past Continuous can describe two actions happening at the same time.

While I was cooking, my friend was setting the table.

Temporary past situation

Use Past Continuous for a temporary situation around a past period.

I was staying with my sister that month.

Decision check:
Finished event / sequence / past fact? → Past Simple.
Action in progress / background / temporary past situation? → Past Continuous.
Background + shorter event? → often Past Continuous + Past Simple.
Remember: Past Continuous does not mean “long action” and Past Simple does not mean “short action.” The key question is whether you see the action as in progress or complete.
