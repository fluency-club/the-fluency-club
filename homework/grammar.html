🧠 Lesson recap — Grammar

Present Perfect

have / has + V3

Use it for a past action that is connected to now: experience, a result now, or unfinished time.

Example: We have visited three cities so far.

Past Perfect

had + V3

Use it for an action that happened before another past action or past moment.

Example: By the time we reached Boston, the hotel had cancelled our room.

Present Perfect Continuous

have / has + been + verb-ing

Use it for an activity that started in the past and continues now, or a recent activity with a present result.

Example: We have been driving for four hours.

Present Perfect vs Continuous

Present Perfect = result / how many / completed result.

Present Perfect Continuous = process / duration / repeated activity.

Compare: I’ve visited 5 museums. / I’ve been sightseeing all day.

Remember: connected to now → Present Perfect. Earlier than another past event → Past Perfect.

5. Reading — The Long Way to California 📖

Emma and her husband have been travelling across the United States for ten days. So far, they have visited New York, Washington, D.C., and Chicago. They have taken hundreds of photos, but Emma says the best part of the trip has been the unexpected moments.

Before they arrived in Chicago, they had planned to stay in a small hotel near the river. However, when they reached the reception desk, they discovered that the hotel had given their room to another guest. The receptionist explained that a computer problem had caused several booking mistakes. Luckily, the manager found them a room in another hotel and paid for their taxi.

Since leaving Chicago, they have been driving west. They have been spending most of the day on the road, but they stop whenever they see something interesting. Yesterday they took a scenic route through the mountains. Emma has already decided that she wants to return next year because they still haven’t seen California.
