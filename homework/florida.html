🧠 Lesson recap — Grammar

Past Perfect

had + V3

Use it for an action completed before another past action or past reference point.

By the time we reached Miami, the storm had already started.

Present Perfect

have / has + V3

Use it for a past action connected to now: experience, result, or unfinished time.

I have visited Florida twice.

Present Perfect Continuous

have / has been + -ing

Use it for duration, repeated activity, or an ongoing/recent process.

We have been driving since sunrise.
Key contrasts:
Past Perfect vs Present Perfect → past reference point vs connection to now.
Present Perfect vs Present Perfect Continuous → result / amount / completion vs process / duration / repetition.
Stative verbs usually stay non-continuous: I’ve known her for years.

5. Reading — A Stormy Florida Weekend 📖

Olivia has been living in Atlanta for three years, but she has always wanted to explore more of Florida. Last weekend, she finally drove south with two friends. Before they left, they had checked the weather forecast several times, and everything had looked perfect.

By the time they reached the Gulf Coast, however, dark clouds had already appeared. They had planned to spend the afternoon on the beach, but a storm forced them to change their itinerary. Since arriving, they have visited a small art museum, tried key lime pie, and found a local seafood restaurant.

The weather has been improving slowly, so they have been spending more time outside. Olivia says she has taken more than 200 photos, while one of her friends has been filming short videos all weekend. Although the trip has not gone exactly as planned, Olivia says it has been one of their most memorable road trips.
