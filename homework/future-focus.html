Make your future plans precise and your message persuasive. Practise meaning, emphasis and context before you check your results.

1. Phrase refresh 💬

Recall the meaning and an example, then tap to reveal. Use the phrases in the tasks below.

2. Future forms — meaning first 🎯

Choose the wording that best matches the stated focus. Several future forms can describe the same event; context determines the intended meaning.

3. Put the emphasis in the right place ⚡

Clefts, fronting and inversion: control both the grammar and the focus.

Rewrite to focus on the person: “Maya changed the plan.”
It was Maya who changed the plan.
Rewrite with Rarely: “I rarely make assumptions about clients.”
Rarely do I make assumptions about clients.
Use fronting to contrast two things: “I can accept the fee, but I cannot accept the delay.”
The fee, I can accept. The delay, I cannot.
Use a what-cleft: “I really want a shared vision.”
What I really want is a shared vision.
