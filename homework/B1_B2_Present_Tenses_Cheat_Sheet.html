Present Tenses Cheat Sheet ⏳

Four present tenses: forms, key uses, signal words and the fastest way to tell them apart.

Present Simple

I/you/we/they work · he/she works

Use: routines, habits, facts, permanent situations, timetables.

I ride twice a week. The lesson starts at 6.

Fast decision: routine/fact → Simple · happening/temporary → Continuous · result/experience → Perfect · activity/duration → Perfect Continuous.

Simple vs Continuous

Simple: normal/repeated/permanent. I work from home.

Continuous: now/temporary/changing. I'm working from a café today.

Trap: a word like today does not automatically mean Continuous.

Perfect vs Perfect Continuous

Perfect: result, completion, experience, number. I've read three chapters.

Perfect Continuous: process or duration. I've been reading for two hours.

How many? often Perfect · How long? often Perfect Continuous.

Since / For

since + starting point: since Monday, since 2024, since I moved here.

for + duration: for two hours, for years, for a long time.

Already / Just / Yet

already = earlier than expected · just = very recently · yet = usually questions/negatives, often at the end.

I've already eaten. She's just left. Have you finished yet?

Stative verbs: usually NOT continuous

-ing spelling

Third-person -s

  • work → works

Unfinished time

today, this week, this month, this year can use Present Perfect when the period is not finished.

I've had three meetings today.

Repeated recent activity

Present Perfect Continuous can show repeated recent actions.

I've been going to the gym a lot lately.

Present Perfect with unfinished time

Use it when the period is still open: I've had two coffees today.

Compare: I had two coffees yesterday. = finished time.

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

Present Perfect: no finished past time or present connection.

Past Simple: finished past time: yesterday, last week, in 2024, two days ago.

Mini decision tree:
1) Routine/fact? → Present Simple.
2) Happening/temporary/changing? → Present Continuous.
3) Experience/result/unfinished time? → Present Perfect.
4) Activity/duration/repeated recent activity? → Present Perfect Continuous.
Remember: signal words help, but meaning decides the tense. The same phrase (for example this week) can work with different tenses depending on what you want to say.
